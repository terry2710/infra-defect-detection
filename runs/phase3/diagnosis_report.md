# Phase 3 diagnosis: why does cross-country performance drop, and by how much

This is the analysis layer on top of `cross_country_report.md` and `failures/failures_report.md`
(both auto-generated by the training/diagnosis scripts) - it cross-references phase 3's results
against phase 1's `data/eda_report.md` to figure out *why* each target country drops, not just *how
much*. One YOLO11n model, trained once on Japan+India+Czech (8536 train images), evaluated on an
in-domain held-out test split and on four countries it never trained on.

## Headline numbers

| domain | n images | mAP@50 | Δ vs in-domain | recall (failure-diagnosis pass) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in_domain (fair baseline) | 1830 | 0.5198 | - | - |
| United_States | 4805 | 0.4316 | -0.088 | 0.51 |
| China_MotorBike | 1934 | 0.2429 | -0.277 | 0.19 |
| China_Drone | 1919 | 0.2268 | -0.293 | 0.15 |
| Norway | 2914 | 0.0686 | **-0.451** | **0.09** |

Norway isn't just the worst - it's in a different category of failure from the other three. Recall
collapses to 9%: the model is effectively blind on Norway, not just "worse than usual."

## The brightness hypothesis (from phase 1) doesn't hold up

Phase 1's EDA flagged brightness as a country-level difference worth watching, and
`diagnose_failures.py` deliberately records per-image brightness so this could be checked. Lining
the two reports up:

| country | role | brightness_mean (phase 1 EDA) |
|---|---|---|
| Japan | train | 108.5 |
| Czech | train | 119.8 |
| India | train | 135.9 |
| **Norway** | **target (worst, -0.45)** | **140.3** |
| China_Drone | target | 147.5 |
| China_MotorBike | target | 150.8 |
| United_States | target (best, -0.09) | 154.5 |

Norway (140.3) is *not* the outlier here - it sits in the middle of the pack, closer to the training
distribution than China_MotorBike or United_States are, and United_States (the target with the
*smallest* drop) is actually the *brightest* country in the whole dataset. If brightness were the
main driver, United_States should be struggling and Norway should be fine. It's the opposite. So for
this dataset, brightness is not what's driving the cross-country gap - a hypothesis phase 1 raised as
plausible, but phase 3's evidence rules it out as the primary cause.

## What actually explains Norway's collapse: image geometry, not lighting

Going back to phase 1's EDA table for the format/geometry columns (not just brightness) makes the
real driver obvious:

| country | resolution(s) | mean aspect ratio | objects per image |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan / India / Czech (train) | single fixed res each (600x600 / 720x720 / 600x600) | 1.00 | 1.6 - 2.1 |
| United_States (target, best) | single fixed res (640x640 - matches training imgsz exactly) | 1.00 | 2.29 |
| China_MotorBike / China_Drone (target) | single fixed res (512x512) | 1.00 | 1.6 - 2.4 |
| **Norway (target, worst)** | **3 distinct resolutions, up to 3643x2041** | **1.83** | **3.85** |

Every training country - and every other target country - is square (aspect ratio 1.00) at a single
fixed resolution. Norway is the only country in the dataset that is neither: it's wide-format
(1.83:1), high-resolution, and shot at multiple resolutions. Training and evaluation both resize to
imgsz=640, so a 3643x2041 Norway image gets squashed to a square 640x640 tile - a much more aggressive
and asymmetric distortion than anything the model saw during training, where every image was already
square. On top of that, Norway scenes average 3.85 damage objects per image versus 1.6-2.4 for every
other country - the model also has to parse noticeably denser scenes than it was ever trained on. Both
of these compound in the same direction (more distortion, more objects to find), which is consistent
with recall collapsing to 9% rather than degrading gracefully like the other three targets.

**Practical implication for anyone reusing this model**: the fix for Norway-like inputs isn't "collect
more data" or "fix the lighting" - it's aspect-ratio-preserving preprocessing (e.g. letterboxing
instead of a naive resize) before feeding non-square, high-res images into a model trained exclusively
on square crops. This is a preprocessing/format mismatch, not a content or lighting domain gap.

## China_MotorBike vs. China_Drone: a genuine content-domain gap, not a format one

Unlike Norway, both China splits *do* match the training countries on format (square, single fixed
resolution, object density in the same range as Japan/India/Czech). Their drops (-0.277 and -0.293)
are close to each other and both worse than United_States but nowhere near Norway's collapse - the
signature of a real visual/content domain shift (different road materials, signage, damage
appearance) rather than a preprocessing artifact. China_Drone drops slightly more than
China_MotorBike (0.227 vs 0.243 mAP@50), which lines up with the project's original reasoning for
keeping these two splits separate instead of merging into one "China" number: a drone's top-down,
higher-altitude viewpoint is an additional shift on top of the country-level content gap that a
motorbike-mounted (road-level, same perspective family as the training data) camera doesn't have.

## United_States transfers best - and its format explains why

United_States has the smallest drop (-0.088) of any target country. It's also the target that most
closely matches the training format: single resolution, square aspect ratio, and its 640x640 native
resolution is an exact match for the training imgsz - so it's the only target country requiring
effectively *no* resize distortion at all. Its brightness (154.5) and object density (2.29/image) are
also both within the training countries' range. Every format-level axis measured here says
United_States should transfer best, and it does.

## One pattern that holds across every target country: pothole recall collapses hardest

Per-class AP50 in `cross_country_report.md` shows pothole is the weakest class in every single target
domain, often by a wide margin - e.g. Norway pothole AP50 = 0.0145 (essentially undetected, vs.
0.09-0.12 for the other three classes there), United_States pothole recall = 0.25 vs. 0.46-0.61 for
the crack classes. This holds regardless of which country-level failure mode (format mismatch for
Norway, content shift for China/US) is in play, which suggests pothole is intrinsically the
hardest-to-generalize class in this dataset - plausibly because potholes are the most visually
heterogeneous damage type (irregular shape, no consistent linear structure the way cracks have) and
so the model's learned prototype for them is the least robust to any kind of distribution shift.

## Summary

| target | drop | primary driver | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norway | -0.451 (catastrophic) | image format/geometry mismatch, not lighting | only non-square, multi-resolution, highest-object-density country; brightness is mid-pack |
| China_Drone | -0.293 | content-domain shift + drone viewpoint | format matches training; drop close to China_MotorBike but slightly worse |
| China_MotorBike | -0.277 | content-domain shift | format matches training; drop close to China_Drone |
| United_States | -0.088 (best) | closest format + content match | native res = training imgsz, square, brightest country, object density in-range |

Cross-cutting: pothole is the weakest class everywhere, independent of which country-level failure
mode applies.
